# Melbourne map for Trainsworld 0.4.0

Import `melbourne-v040-rail-and-trams.save` with **Load a file**. It keeps the 16 simplified rail services and 24 weekday tram routes from the previous revision. The [earlier tram report](TRAMS.md) documents the timetable simplifications, train and tram length estimates, selected service day and the rest of the source provenance.

## Upstream update

This branch merges upstream Trainsworld commit `8ebd2d2`, which includes the published v0.4.0 changelog. Relevant changes include terminal and passing-track upgrades for tram stops, fixes for single-track and one-way stations, narrower tram-stop rendering, a timetable and weekend scheduling, visibility controls, and a real-network map that includes unused track. The fork's `main` was fast-forwarded to this upstream commit; the Melbourne generator remains on its separate feature branch. Weekend schedules have not been reconstructed for this save.

## Tram and rail changes

The generator uses the game's **18 November 2026** Australian OSM-derived real-network graph as a source of tram and light-rail geometry, cropped in memory to the Melbourne stop area. It uses OSM routing for **843 of 843 distinct selected tram stop-to-stop hops**, including the City Circle connection that previously fell back to a straight line. Stops are snapped within 20 m for matching and routes must pass distance and GTFS-shape plausibility checks; the resulting game curves are approximations and are not guaranteed to coincide exactly with road lanes or OSM track. The tighter tram trace and station approaches reduce the sweeping curves seen around the street grid. There is still no separate road-snap operation.

The builder no longer creates extra tram station platforms purely to meet demand. Separate station nodes can remain where distinct track pairs or approaches require them: 22 stop names have two nodes and none has more than two in this build. It retains the earlier two-pair approximation on 17 busy Swanston Street sections. The v0.4 terminal and passing-track upgrades are applied at the Flinders Street/Elizabeth Street and Waterfront City tram termini instead of multiplying their station nodes. This is an operating approximation, not a claim that both real stops have identical infrastructure.

The four Loop groups have wider diagrammatic spacing, including three parallel routes between Flagstaff and Southern Cross and four between Southern Cross and Flinders Street. The Northern Group takes its existing North Melbourne approach rather than the Flagstaff–Southern Cross segment. Several approaches were shifted sideways to reduce near-coincident tracks; two exactly identical generated rail edges were merged so their services use the same game edge. The Clifton Hill level transition was moved away from Flinders Street and Southern Cross platforms. These changes preserve the existing service paths, not an exact survey of platform locations or railway elevations. Some overlapping or crossing approaches remain, especially around the CBD and Richmond, and need a further in-game review.

## Validation and remaining limitations

The v0.4 native loader and service model reload **40 services as running**, including all 24 tram numbers. Automated checks cover each built, connected route, City Loop and Metro Tunnel through paths, the City Circle loop, tram track type, green colour, tram fleet, the two upgraded tram termini, and separation of event and cross-city game edges. The result has **1,057 distinct station and stop names**, 1,307 edges, 1,223 rail cars and 696 tram sections. Fifteen Python tests and 18 upstream tram tests pass (one upstream test ignored).

The game's geometry validator reports **22 warnings**: eight tight-radius issues, five node headings, one one-sided node, seven platform joints and one platform junction. All services ran in the native model, but these warnings may affect appearance and need attention. The model's capacity diagnostic has one terminus above 90% utilisation in the high-demand level, at Flinders Street/Elizabeth Street for routes 19, 57 and 59; delays are still possible. Service frequency and capacity are approximations, and the output has not been visually inspected in a WebGL-capable game session. The City Circle has no trips in the selected high-demand windows and therefore is not dispatched at the initial 07:00 clock time.

## Rebuild

Use the pinned GTFS archive, Melbourne pack, and **13 November 2026 rail graph** in [METRO.md](METRO.md), plus the [18 November 2026 Australian graph](https://data.anita.garden/trainworld/2026-11-18/realgraph/au.rgr) for tram geometry (SHA-256 `5ed852b014bc36577188ec03be2fadee049851f0a3f18cfb07ae147ce6889d68`). The graph is derived from OpenStreetMap; © OpenStreetMap contributors, [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Its exact underlying OSM extract date has not been independently verified. Only the Melbourne-area graph is routed; no worldwide dataset regeneration is required.

```sh
python melbourne/metro_network.py --simplified --city-loops --trams \
  --gtfs /path/to/gtfs.zip --date 2026-11-18 --event-date 2026-11-21 \
  --pack /path/to/mel.json --graph /path/to/au-2026-11-13.rgr \
  --tram-graph /path/to/au-2026-11-18.rgr --out /path/to/new-build-directory
python -m unittest discover -s melbourne -v
python melbourne/validate_metro.py /path/to/new-build-directory/mel_real.save --city-loops --trams --tram-upgrades
```

The generator validates its save before returning. Save SHA-256: `2f941474a9d55ea77cdcb83ead008304227147bc920c0a893039baa30b06543f`.
